## Where the relationship is decided

{The moments of truth — the small number of stages carrying most of the outcome, with what
makes each one decisive. Attention spread evenly across a journey is attention wasted.}

## Where it hurts, and what it costs the customer

{The pain points that matter, in the customer's terms: their time, their calls, their
uncertainty, the risk to their own operation.}

{Resist restating these as organizational costs. The moment a pain point becomes "handling
cost" it stops being about the customer and starts competing for budget on the wrong
argument.}

## What the organization cannot see

{Stages producing no record. This is a finding about the organization rather than a gap in
this map: a stage with no data appears in no dashboard, so it is on no improvement list, and
it will not appear on one by itself.}

{Name what it would take to see them — instrumentation, or simply somebody asking.}

## Where the customer meets more than one organization

{Fragmentation: information asked for twice, answers that differ by channel, the customer
having to know which system holds the truth. The customer does not care about the seams,
and the seams are where the pain is.}

## What happens when it goes wrong

{Recovery, by stage. Where a customer hitting a problem has no route to resolution, say so
plainly — customers forgive failures and leave over how they were handled.}

## Where segments differ

{Only where segments were mapped separately. Where they were not, state what the averaged
map is likely concealing — usually that the largest and smallest accounts are having
completely different experiences of the same organization.}

## Deliberately not decided here

- **Which systems to replace, consolidate, or retire.** Pain at a system is a finding; the
  disposition is a portfolio decision made on evidence this method never sees.
- The architecture of any replacement.
- How work flows internally between the teams the customer never sees.
- Whether fixing any of this pays for itself.
- Whether a pain point is worth fixing. This says what it costs the customer.
