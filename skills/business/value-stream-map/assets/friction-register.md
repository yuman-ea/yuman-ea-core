# Friction register — {stream name}

**Confidence:** {low | medium | high}

> **Friction is located at the handoff, not inside a stage.** Recording it inside one assigns
> it to a team that cannot fix it alone — which is how it has survived every previous round
> of improvement.

## The register

| # | Located at | Type | Elapsed cost | Frequency | Root stage | Owned by | Who must agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | {between stage 3 and stage 4} | {queue \| rework_loop \| re_keying \| approval_wait \| information_gap \| system_boundary \| function_boundary \| unclear_ownership} | {…} | {…} | {where the CAUSE is} | {name \| **nobody**} | {…} |

*The **root stage** is frequently the stage before the one where the problem appears. Work
arriving wrong at stage 4 is usually a stage 3 problem, and pointing the improvement at
stage 4 is the most common way effort here is wasted.*

## Friction at a system boundary

| # | Between | What crosses | How it crosses | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| {…} | {system A → system B} | {…} | {interface \| **re-keyed by hand** \| spreadsheet \| email \| phone} | {…} |

*Where system touchpoints were inferred from the inventory and interface map, only the
automated crossings are visible. The manual ones — re-keying, spreadsheets, email — are
disproportionately where the friction is and will be under-represented here. Say so.*

## What the register does not claim

- That any of these should be fixed. It says what they cost and who would have to agree.
- That fixing them speeds up the stream. **Only the constraint does that** — the rest reduce
  effort, error, or irritation, which are worth having and are not the same thing.
